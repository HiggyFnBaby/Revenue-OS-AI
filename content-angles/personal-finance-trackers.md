# Content Angles: Personal Finance Trackers (Higgyd Money OS)

**Agent:** content-angle-strategist
**Inputs:** `../signal-reports/personal-finance-trackers.md`,
`../offer-briefs/personal-finance-trackers.md`

Every angle below leads toward the offer brief's promise — **"find the
subscription money you're already leaking in week one, or it's free"** —
$7/mo, no annual lock-in. None hard-sell in the hook itself; the bridge
happens after the hook lands.

---

## 1. "$133 you don't know you're losing" (COLD)

- **Hook:** *"The average person wastes $133 a year on subscriptions they
  forgot to cancel. You almost certainly don't know your number."*
- **Funnel stage:** Cold — works on anyone, no prior awareness of budgeting
  apps required. It's a stat designed to make the reader wonder about their
  own number before they know a product exists to answer it.
- **Bridge to offer:** Once they're curious about their own number, the
  follow-through is "most trackers show you what you spent — this one tells
  you what you're wasting" — leads into the offer's core mechanism without
  naming price yet.
- **Format:** Social post / video hook (first 2 seconds).

## 2. "You don't have a spending problem" (COLD)

- **Hook:** *"You don't have a spending problem. You have a subscription you
  forgot about three renewals ago."*
- **Funnel stage:** Cold — reframes budgeting-app-avoidance guilt ("I'm bad
  with money") as a structural, findable problem instead of a willpower
  problem — same reframe pattern the signal report's evidence supports
  (people don't cancel because checkout is frictionless and cancellation
  isn't, not because they're careless).
- **Bridge to offer:** Structural leaks need something that finds them
  automatically, not more self-discipline — bridges into "here's what finds
  it" without describing it as a product/price yet.
- **Format:** Social post, or comment reply on a post where someone's
  venting about their budget/spending.

## 3. "Still 'temporarily' using Credit Karma?" (WARM)

- **Hook:** *"Credit Karma dropped custom budgets and categories when Mint
  shut down. If you're still 'temporarily' making it work two years later,
  it's not temporary."*
- **Funnel stage:** Warm — targets people who already know their current
  setup doesn't really work (report #1: Credit Karma's budgeting is
  "too basic," "nothing close to what Mint offers") but haven't gone looking
  for a real replacement because the acute anger has faded. Names the
  specific, evidenced gap rather than a generic "switch trackers" pitch.
- **Bridge to offer:** "A real replacement should do more than track — it
  should tell you what to stop paying for" — bridges into the offer's
  subscription-waste mechanism as the differentiator vs. just being
  "Mint again."
- **Format:** Comment reply on posts/threads where someone mentions still
  using Credit Karma or Mint alternatives; DM opener in budgeting groups.

## 4. "When's the last time you actually looked?" (WARM)

- **Hook:** *"When's the last time you actually read your full subscription
  list, not just the ones you remember?"*
- **Funnel stage:** Warm — aimed at people who know subscription creep is a
  thing (it's common, named advice: "audit your subscriptions regularly")
  but haven't done it because it's tedious across scattered statements. The
  question format makes them self-assess rather than being told they have a
  problem.
- **Bridge to offer:** The honest answer for almost everyone is "not
  recently, it's a pain to check" — bridges into "that's exactly what this
  finds automatically, in a week."
- **Format:** Poll-style social post, or DM opener in budgeting/frugality
  communities.

## 5. "Why pay someone 40% of your own money back?" (HOT) — test first

- **Hook:** *"Rocket Money takes 35–60% of whatever it finds you in
  savings. What if you just found it yourself, for $7 flat?"*
- **Funnel stage:** Hot — aimed squarely at people already using or
  aware of subscription-audit/bill-negotiation services, actively comparing
  options. This is the angle written closest to the signal report's actual
  evidence language (Rocket Money's fee structure and the BBB complaints
  about being charged twice on unchanged savings).
- **Bridge to offer:** Directly into the offer's sharpest, most evidenced
  contrast: flat $7/mo vs. a cut of their own found money, plus the full
  tracker Rocket Money's point-solution doesn't give them.
- **Format:** Comment reply on posts about subscription/bill-negotiation
  apps; DM opener targeted at Rocket Money complaint threads.
- **Why test this one first:** the signal report rates this pain point's
  willingness-to-pay evidence as the strongest and most direct in the whole
  report — people already paying a steep percentage fee for a narrower
  version of this exact value are the most primed, highest-intent audience
  available, same logic the real-estate-agents brief used to pick its
  kvCORE-contrast angle first.

## 6. "Best $9/month I spend" — but for people who never got that far (HOT)

- **Hook:** *"'Incredibly over complicated... only if you have someone on
  staff to take all the tutorials.' Sound familiar? Some people say YNAB
  becomes 'the best $9/month I spend' — most never make it that far."*
- **Funnel stage:** Hot — targets people actively shopping or who
  rage-quit a zero-based-budgeting tool like YNAB over complexity (report
  #4's real quotes, both the complaint and the eventual-convert side),
  without asking them to adopt YNAB's specific method.
- **Bridge to offer:** The value the retained YNAB users describe (seeing
  where money actually goes) doesn't require the learning curve that makes
  others quit — bridges into "same clarity, none of the setup."
- **Format:** Comment reply on posts/reviews mentioning YNAB's learning
  curve or price; social post targeting "YNAB alternative" search intent.

---

## Recommendation

**Test #5 first.** It has the strongest signal-to-offer traceability (built
almost word-for-word from the report's single strongest willingness-to-pay
evidence) and reaches people already paying real money — a steep percentage
fee — for a narrower version of the same value. Run #1 and #2 in parallel as
top-of-funnel angles to build a pipeline behind #5, since #5 alone only
reaches people who've already found (and are already paying for) a partial
fix — it won't reach the much larger group who haven't looked yet.
